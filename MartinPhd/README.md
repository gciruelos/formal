# F. D. Martin, *Aspectos homológicos de la teoría de especies*

PhD thesis, Universidad de Buenos Aires, 2024 (advisor: Mariano Suárez-Álvarez).
Source: [`MartinPhd.pdf`](MartinPhd.pdf). Build: `lake build MartinPhd` from the project root.

Chapter 1 computes the cohomology of the species of flags over a finite field and shows that
complete flags have the same cohomology as linear orders: a q-analogue of a theorem on
combinatorial species.

## Main results

1. **Theorem 1.7 (Solomon–Tits).** The Tits building of `Fⁿ` has reduced homology only in
   degree `n − 2`. That homology is the Steinberg module, of dimension `q^{n(n-1)/2}`.
2. **Theorem 1.35.** The cohomology algebra of the species of all flags is `Λ[x] ⊗ k[y]`, with
   `x` in degree 1 and `y` in degree 2.
3. **Theorem 1.36.** Restricting from all flags to complete flags is an isomorphism of
   cohomology algebras.

## The results in brief

A *combinatorial species* (Joyal) assigns a vector space to every finite set, functorially in
bijections: for instance, the species of linear orders sends a set to the span of its
orderings. A *GL-species* replaces finite sets by finite-dimensional vector spaces over a
finite field `F = 𝔽_q`, and bijections by linear isomorphisms. Results about GL-species are
q-analogues of results about species: as `q → 1`, subspaces become subsets and flags of
subspaces become chains of subsets. In this dictionary, complete flags (chains with one
subspace of each dimension) play the role of linear orders.

Both settings have an *exponential species* `e`, which is the unit for the relevant product.
A species with compatible restriction and quotient maps to subspaces is an `e`-bicomodule,
and its cohomology `H•(x) = Ext•(x, e)` can be computed by an explicit cobar complex of
cochains on flags. For combinatorial species, Aguiar–Mahajan and Tamaroff computed this
cohomology for linear orders and set compositions. Martin does the same over `𝔽_q`.

* **Solomon–Tits** is the geometric input. The poset of proper nonzero subspaces of `Fⁿ` (the
  Tits building) has the homology of a wedge of `q^{n(n-1)/2}` spheres of dimension `n − 2`.
  Its top homology, the Steinberg representation of `GL_n(𝔽_q)`, is spanned by alternating
  sums of flags built from permutations. This controls the first page of the spectral
  sequence used later.
* **Theorem 1.35** says that the cohomology of the species `Σ` of *all* flags is
  one-dimensional in every degree. The cohomology is generated by two classes: a
  degree-1 *dimension class* `x`, which squares to zero, and a degree-2 *Schubert class* `y`,
  built from inversions of relative positions of flags (the Bruhat decomposition). The
  proof filters the cobar complex by flag length and computes the first page of the spectral
  sequence with Solomon–Tits and Bruhat-cell counting (`q`-multinomials).
* **Theorem 1.36** is the chapter's headline. The species of *complete* flags has the same
  cohomology algebra as all flags, so (via 1.35) the same as the species of linear orders in
  the classical case. The q-analogue therefore loses no cohomological information. The proof
  compares the two spectral sequences with the comparison theorem for multiplicative spectral
  sequences.
